A stimulated echo

(a) Delay the third pulse. Which marker moves with it, and by how much?

Three 90° pulses: the first on its own at t = 0, then one per arrow press, whenever you like (spaced out, say near 20 and 50 ms, the echoes stand apart). Markers show every echo they will make: spin echoes SE, and the stimulated echo STE.

Three 90° pulses, the last two fired by the arrow keys whenever the class likes; pulses near 20 and 50 ms keep the echoes apart (the fan is a bit slower on this slide, ±200° by 10 ms, and the clock runs at 4 ms per second). After the second 90°, part of the fanned-out signal is tipped onto z, where it keeps a record of each spin’s phase (as a pattern of Mz) and only relaxes with T1. The third 90° brings it back, mirrored, and it refocuses τ after that pulse, where τ is the gap between the first two: the stimulated echo (STE). It is at most half of the signal. The spin echoes (SE) come at twice the spacings: 2·t2 (pulses 1 and 2), 2·t3 − 2·t2 (refocused again by the third pulse), 2·t3 (pulses 1 and 3). Try waiting longer before the third pulse: the STE moves with it, always τ later, and barely shrinks (T1 = 1000 ms), while the spin echoes fade with T2 = 80 ms.

Three pulses also make a stimulated echo

  • It comes τ after the third pulse: delay that pulse and the STE follows it, while the spin echoes move twice as far.
  • It waits along z, relaxing with T1; with 90° pulses it carries up to half the signal.
Lesson of the stimulated echo slide. The next slide compares the two echoes side by side. In full: The second 90° parks part of the fanned-out signal along z, keeping each spin’s phase as a pattern of Mz. The third 90° brings it back, and it refocuses τ after that pulse (τ = the gap between the first two): the stimulated echo. While parked along z it relaxes with T1, not T2, so it can outlast the spin echoes. With 90° pulses it carries up to half of the signal; the spin echoes come at the mirror times as before.

An imperfect 180° is part 180°, part 90°

(b) Lower the angle from 180°. What appears, and from which pulse on?

Set the angle, press Play, then fire two refocusing pulses at that angle, whenever you like. At 180° only spin echoes come back; lower the angle and stimulated echoes appear.

A refocusing pulse of angle α acts on the fanned-out transverse signal in three ways at once: it refocuses a share sin²(α/2) (like a 180°: spin echo), leaves cos²(α/2) alone (like a 0°), and tips part onto z (like a 90°: ½ sin α of it). What it tips onto z, the next pulse’s 90° part brings back as a stimulated echo. At 180° the bars read 100%, 0%, 0%: only spin echoes. At 160°: 97%, 3%, 17%. At 120°: 75%, 25%, 43%. At 90°: 50%, 50%, 50%, the stimulated-echo slide. The marker sizes follow the same rules, with T2 = 80 ms while transverse and T1 = 1000 ms along z. This is why a train of imperfect 180°s (Exercise 2-3) has stimulated echoes in every echo after the first.

What we saw · 2

An imperfect 180° is part 180°, part 90°

  • Its 90° part parks signal along z; the next pulse brings it back as a stimulated echo.
  • Only a perfect 180° gives pure spin echoes.
Lesson of the imperfect-180° slide. B1 inhomogeneity and slice profiles make refocusing angles below 180° the norm in practice. In full: A refocusing pulse of angle α acts like a 180° on sin²(α/2) of the signal, like a 0° on cos²(α/2), and like a 90° on ½ sin α. Its 90° part parks signal along z, and the next pulse brings it back: from the second refocusing pulse on, stimulated echoes appear. At 160°: 97% refocused, 3% left alone, 17% to z. At 120°: 75%, 25%, 43%. Only a perfect 180° gives pure spin echoes.

Evenly spaced: the echoes line up

(c) What happens to the spin and stimulated echoes when the pulses are evenly spaced?

The bridge to Exercise 2-3. With the 90° at 0 and refocusing pulses at τ, 3τ, 5τ (τ = 10 ms), every spin echo and every stimulated echo forms at 2τ, 4τ, 6τ: they add up in the same echo, and the signal measured at each echo time is a mix of both (amber markers, SE+STE). That is a multi-echo spin echo (CPMG) train with imperfect refocusing pulses, the setting of Exercise 2-3. Timed by hand, the pulses are never quite even and the echoes split apart again.

What we saw · 3

Evenly spaced, the echoes coincide

  • In an echo train, each echo mixes spin and stimulated echoes.
  • Their parts can add or partly cancel.
Lesson of the evenly spaced slide, and the bridge to Exercise 2-3. In full: With refocusing pulses at τ, 3τ, 5τ, …, every spin echo and every stimulated echo forms at 2τ, 4τ, 6τ, … So each echo of a multi-echo spin echo train is a mix of spin and stimulated echoes, unless the refocusing angle is exactly 180°. The parts meeting in one echo can add or partly cancel, depending on their phases: with 120° pulses at 10, 30 and 50 ms, the echo at 60 ms is 42%, not the 70% their sizes add up to. This mix is what Exercise 2-3 measures, and why it biases T2.
