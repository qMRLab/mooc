Measurements and the T2 fit

Try any combination of directions: arbitrary, always the same, alternating. Do the results change?

(d) Then, with the Lorentzian fan, play without any 180° pulses at all, and fit. What value did you get?

The ten echo amplitudes from the game, shown first on their own; Fit then adds a mono-exponential fit S0·e^(−TE/T2) (least squares on the amplitudes, so missed echoes count as low points rather than breaking the fit), the fitted T2, and how far it is from the true 80 ms. Once the fit is shown, Hint suggests trying other combinations of pulse directions (arbitrary, always the same, alternating): with perfect pulses the results do not change, only the timing matters. Its second suggestion, the Lorentzian fan with no 180° pulses at all: the fit then gives T2*, not T2. A Lorentzian spread of frequencies makes the signal decay as e^(−t/T2′), here with T2′ ≈ 19 ms, so 1/T2* = 1/T2 + 1/T2′ gives T2* ≈ 15.4 ms (the fit gives about 15.6 ms). The other fans do not give a clean T2*: the equally spaced fan decays as a sinc, with ripples, and the Gaussian fan as a Gaussian, so an exponential fit to them means little. What we saw · 1

Perfect pulses give T2 exactly

  • Pure spin echoes: the fit gives 80.0 ms.
  • Only the timing matters, not the pulse axis.
  • Without any refocusing pulses, the fit with the Lorentzian fan gives T2* (about 15 ms), not T2.
Lesson of the first game and its fit. In full: With a 90° and 180°s, every echo is a pure spin echo: the train follows e−TE/T2, and the fit gives 80.0 ms. The axis of the 180°s does not matter yet: CP (→) and CPMG (↑) give the same echoes. Timing does: a pulse off the beat moves its echo away from the acquisition, and the measurement drops.

With CPMG phases the train stays clean but the fit comes out a little long (about 81 ms when every pulse is on the beat): part of the signal spends time along z, where nothing decays here (T1 infinite). What we saw · 2

The CPMG condition

  • Refocus about the axis M lies on after the 90° (+y after a 90° about +x): each pulse’s error is undone by the next, and the train stays clean.
  • CPMG (↑): about 81 ms, slightly long from stimulated echoes. CP (→): the train falls apart.
Lesson of the B1 game with crushers. In full: Crushers spoil what a refocusing pulse tips into the plane from magnetization that was never transverse: the 80° pulse’s leftover. Stimulated echoes survive: their magnetization carries the crusher’s phase and is untwisted by the next lobe. The bias drops from about +3 ms to about +1 ms (81.4 ms), and dropping echo 1 brings it to about +0.1 ms (80.1 ms).

(c) Which echoes would you fit? After the first fit, try each option.

Go back to the game and turn the 3D view to the z–x plane (or pick Side: z–x plane in the view menu). Can you see why fitting every even echo gives a closer T2 than every odd echo?

The ten measurements from the 60°/120° game with crushers, shown first on their own; Fit adds S0·e^(−TE/T2), the fitted T2 and its difference from the true 80 ms. Then the fit can be redone without echo 1, on the even echoes only, or on the odd echoes only, one at a time, with the echoes left out greyed; "Fit all echoes" goes back. Without echo 1: echo 1 is the spin echo alone (sin²(60°) = 75% of what a 180° would give), while from echo 2 on, stimulated echoes add in, so dropping echo 1 removes most of the bias (about 93 → 84 ms with CPMG on the beat). Even or odd echoes only: the CPMG train zig-zags between odd and even echoes, and each subset is biased its own way. None of these simple fixes removes the bias at 120°; Milford et al. 2015 (PLOS ONE) and McPhee & Wilman 2018 (JMRI) report the same, and recommend fitting a model of the sequence (EPG, Bloch simulation). Hint: in the z–x side view (the pulses are about +y, so they tip the fanned-out spins between x and z), at echo 1 about a third of the magnetization is still tipped out along z (mean |Mz| ≈ 0.3), so echo 1 is low (about 84% of ideal); the second pulse tips it back and echo 2 is on the curve (about 101%). The odd echoes include echo 1, the lowest point, which pulls the odd fit to about 95 ms; without echo 1 the odd echoes give about 87 ms, close to the even ones (about 84 ms). In this game the later echoes do not alternate much: echo 1 makes most of the odd–even difference. Try again goes back to that game and resets it. Export saves the run as JSON.

What we saw · 3

Big B1 errors: no simple fit fixes it

  • All echoes about 93 ms; without echo 1 about 84; even echoes about 84.
  • The fix: fit a model that includes stimulated echoes, such as an extended phase graph (EPG) simulation of the echo train.
Lesson of the stronger B1 game. Milford et al. 2015 (PLOS ONE) and McPhee & Wilman 2018 (JMRI) report the same with simulations and phantoms. In full: At 60° and 120°, the CPMG train zig-zags between odd and even echoes, and the fit gives about 93 ms. Dropping echo 1 (about 84 ms) or fitting only the even echoes (about 84 ms) helps; fitting only the odd echoes makes it worse (about 95 ms). Echo 1 is the biggest outlier (a spin echo alone), but every later echo carries stimulated echoes too. The robust fix is to fit a model of the sequence that includes stimulated echoes (extended phase graphs; Milford et al. 2015, McPhee & Wilman 2018).

Lessons

Measuring T2 with an echo train

  1. Perfect pulses: T2 exactly, whatever the phase directions.
  2. The CPMG condition: refocus about the axis M lies on after the 90°; CP falls apart with imperfect pulses.
  3. Large B1 errors: dropping echo 1 or fitting even echoes helps, but not enough.
  4. The fix: model the stimulated echoes in the fit (extended phase graph, EPG).
  5. No refocusing: the signal decays with T2*, not T2.
