a. Optimal TE for T2 contrast

a. Using Figure 3.3, determine the approximate optimal echo time for T2 contrast between white matter and gray matter.

The best contrast is where the two curves are furthest apart.

The WM/GM contrast peaks at TE ≈ 103 ms.

Setting the derivative of the difference of the two curves (Eq. 3.1) to zero:

$$\begin{aligned} &\frac{d}{dTE}\left(e^{-\frac{TE}{T_{2,WM}}} - e^{-\frac{TE}{T_{2,GM}}}\right) = 0 \\ &\Rightarrow\; TE_{best} = \frac{T_{2,WM}\,T_{2,GM}}{T_{2,WM}-T_{2,GM}}\ln\frac{T_{2,WM}}{T_{2,GM}} \end{aligned}$$

The slide opens on the book figure with the T2 curves. Three clicks. 1: adds the WM/GM contrast, the difference between the two curves, on its own axis (right): it is small, at most about 0.05 of the signal at TE = 0. 2: marks its maximum, TE ≈ 103 ms for T2 of 109.77 and 96.07 ms. The curve is flat around the peak: anything from about 60 to 160 ms gives at least 90% of the best contrast. 3: the proof, as for the inversion time in the T1 exercises: setting the derivative to zero gives a closed form that sits between the two T2 values.

b. Optimal TE for T2* contrast

b. Repeat for the T2* signal curves.

The WM/GM contrast peaks at TE ≈ 57 ms.

The same formula, with the T2* values. T2* decay is faster, so the best TE is shorter, and the two tissues’ values differ more (by 40% of GM’s, against 14% for T2), so the contrast is larger.

The slide opens on the same figure with its menu set to T2*. Three clicks. 1: the contrast, as in part a. 2: its maximum, TE ≈ 57 ms for T2* of 67.63 and 48.48 ms. 3: compared with part a: the best TE is about half as long, and the contrast is more than twice as large (0.12 against 0.05), because the T2* values differ by 40% of the GM value against 14% for T2.

c. Close to the optimum, not on it

c. Explain why that value coincides or doesn’t with the optimal TE you found above.

  • Flat peak: 128 ms keeps 97% of the best contrast. Only 4 of the echoes are shown; 128 ms is the nearest.
  • 12.8 ms: little decay, mostly proton-density weighted. 256 and 384 ms: less contrast, and only 10% and 3% of the signal left.
  • The optimum depends on the tissues’ T2 (and the field strength): 103 ms is for Figure 3.3’s values.
The slide opens on Figure 3.3 with the WM/GM contrast of part a. Two clicks. 1: Figure 3.1's four TEs on the contrast curve: 128 ms is just past the peak, where the curve is flat. 2: why. The contrast-to-noise ratio peaks at the same TE when the noise is the same in every echo, so the best contrast is also the best CNR. 12.8 ms barely weights T2; 256 and 384 ms are past the peak and have little signal left, so they are noisy, with only fluid still bright. Clinical T2-weighted scans typically use TEs around 80–120 ms for these reasons.
