Lowest useful SNR
  • Later echoes add time (and SAR in a spin-echo train), not information.
  • On magnitude images they sit on a noise floor, which a fit reads as slow decay: T2 is overestimated.
  • 4× the averages adds only T2 ln 2: 76 and 21 ms.
The slide opens on the SNR against TE, on a log scale, for T2 = 109 ms and T2* = 30 ms, with SNR 100 at TE = 0: straight lines, the slope set by T2. Click 1: with SNR 5 as the lowest useful value (the Rose criterion for seeing a feature; change it if the class prefers another), the limit is TE = T2 ln(SNR0/SNRmin): about 330 ms for T2 (about 3 T2) and 90 ms for T2*. Click 2: what it means for the protocol. Plan the echoes for the T2 you expect, up to about 3 T2. Noise-only echoes cost time (and SAR for a spin-echo train), and with magnitude data the Rician noise floor biases the fit towards slower decay. More averages raise SNR0 only by the square root of their number, so the limit moves only logarithmically.
