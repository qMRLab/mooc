A fan of spins, TE/2 after the 90°

Question: which pulse brings the spins back together at TE?

The 90° about +x tipped M onto −y. A time TE/2 has passed since: the spins have fanned out by about 60°, the red ones precessing faster and the blue ones slower. The black arrow, M, is their sum.

A fan of 25 spins, TE/2 after a 90° about +x: their phases are Gaussian with σ = 15° (about 60° from edge to edge), red for the spins that precess faster than the centre frequency, blue for the slower ones. The flip angle is set on the slider (180° to start), and each arrow key applies it about ±x or ±y, animated, with the scan clock stopped (the pulse is instantaneous: no precession during it); Wait TE/2 lets the spins precess for TE/2 (each gains the same phase as in the first TE/2), and Wait TE for twice that, and |Mxy| is the size of their sum in the transverse plane. With 180° about ±x, the fan is mirrored onto +y and comes back together there at TE (|Mxy| back to 100%); with 180° about ±y, it is mirrored about the y axis and refocuses on −y. Below 180°, part of each spin is left behind or tipped out of the plane, and the echo at TE is smaller. Waiting longer after the echo, the fan spreads out again. No relaxation, so the only loss is from the pulse.

What we saw · 1

A pulse’s phase is the axis M turns around

  • Perpendicular to the axis, M sweeps a circle; otherwise, a cone.
  • Along the axis, M doesn’t move.
Lesson of the controls slide: the pulse phase (+x, −x, +y, −y) is the rotation axis. The cone is the key picture for what follows: a pulse acts differently on magnetization depending on where M sits relative to the pulse axis, which is why the axis of the refocusing pulses matters once they are not exactly 180° (CP versus CPMG in Exercise 2-3). In full: The phase sets where B1 points in the transverse plane: +x, −x, +y or −y. M turns around that axis (right-hand rule); −x turns it the opposite way to +x. Same angle, different axis, different result: from +z, 90° about +x puts M on −y; 90° about +y puts it on +x. M sweeps a great circle only when it is perpendicular to the axis. Otherwise it sweeps a cone around the axis: after 45° about +x, M is halfway between +z and −y, and a pulse about +y swings its tip around a cone. M along the axis doesn’t move at all: after 90° about +x, M is on −y, and a pulse about ±y leaves it there. (Exercise 2-3 comes back to this.)

What we saw · 2

A 180° refocuses the fan

  • The echo forms at TE = 2 × t180.
  • Only T2 decay is lost: e−TE/T2.
Lesson of the spin echo slide. The spread of frequencies (the fan) is reversible and is undone by the 180°; T2 decay is not. In full: After the 90°, spins at slightly different frequencies fan out and the signal fades. The 180° flips the fan: spins that were ahead are now behind, so they come back together. The echo forms as long after the 180° as the 180° came after the 90°: TE = 2 × t180. The echo is smaller only because of T2, e−TE/T2: the 180° undoes the fanning out, not the T2 decay.
