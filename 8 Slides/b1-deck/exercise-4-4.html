Exercise 4-4 · (a) · What we saw

Far off resonance, the pulse barely tips M

  • In the frame turning with the RF, the spins feel a static effective field: B1 along x′, plus −Δω/γ along z, what is left of B0 in that frame.
  • 4 kHz off, Δω/γ = 94 µT while B1 peaks at 10 µT: Beff leans only 6° from −z. M along z circles it in a narrow cone, and since the pulse rises and falls slowly, M follows Beff back to z: it ends tipped by 0.03°.
  • On resonance the same pulse turns M by about 870°; at 250 Hz Beff leans 60° and M ends far from z. A Bloch–Siegert pulse must be far enough off resonance to excite nothing, including spins at their own B0 offset.

If this off-resonance pulse tips the longitudinal magnetization of the on-resonance spins, that tip is measured relative to the z axis of which frame?

The Larmor and RF frames turn about z, at f0 and at fRF: all three frames share one z axis (along B0). So the angle from z, and Mz, are the same in every frame, as in the three frame tabs of part (a).

The frames differ only in where x and y point. That matters for the phase: next question.

Each pixel is the angle of Mxy. Measured from the x axis of which frame?

The receiver demodulates at f0: the phase is Mxy’s angle from x′ of the frame turning at f0, the Larmor frame of the on-resonance spins.

  • Not the lab: Mxy spins at 128 MHz there.
  • Not the transmit frame: the scans transmit at f0 + 4 kHz and f0 − 4 kHz, two different frames, but share one receive frame: so their phases can be subtracted.
  • Not the same in all three: z is shared, but each frame’s x turns at its own rate.

Exercise 4-4 · Summary

What the spins showed

  1. Far off resonance, a strong pulse barely tips M: it sees an effective field almost along z (6° from it at 4 kHz, 10 µT).
  2. But it adds a phase: while the pulse is on, transverse M precesses a little away from the RF frequency, and gains φBS = KBS·B1² in the receive frame. The magnitude is untouched.
  3. φBS grows as B1²/offset: twice the B1, four times the phase; twice the offset, half (as long as B1 ≪ the offset). KBS = ∫(γB1,norm(t))²/2ωRF dt is set by the pulse shape and offset: known in advance.
  4. A real image phase also holds the excitation and receive-coil phase, and each spin’s B0 offset. How do you get B1 out?

    Repeat with the pulse on the other side of resonance: the Bloch–Siegert phase flips sign, and nothing else changes. So |φ+ − φ−| = 2KBSB1², and B1 = √(|φ+ − φ−| / 2KBS). Unlike DAM or AFI, T1 and the excitation flip angle don’t enter.
