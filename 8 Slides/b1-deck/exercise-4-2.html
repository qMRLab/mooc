Notice

Every B1 technique that uses magnitude images depends on a ratio of measurements:

DAM: S2α / Sα

AFI: S2 / S1

Why are the receive coils not a problem if the subject doesn’t move, but a problem if they do?

The receive field R multiplies each image, at each piece of brain:

$$S_{\alpha} = R_1\,M_0\sin(\alpha B_1),\quad S_{2\alpha} = R_2\,M_0\sin(2\alpha B_1)\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;\frac{S_{2\alpha}}{2\,S_{\alpha}} = \frac{R_2}{R_1}\cos(\alpha B_1)$$

No movement: R2 = R1, and it cancels. Movement: that piece of brain sat elsewhere in the coils’ field, R2 ≠ R1, and R2/R1 ends up in B1.

Exercise 4-2 · (c) · open question

How can we mitigate this in subjects who have difficulty staying still (e.g. pediatric, Parkinson’s)?

One solution: AFI

  • A double angle takes two separate scans, minutes apart: the more the subject moves, the more R2/R1 ends up in B1, and registration can’t remove it.
  • AFI acquires its two images in the same scan: each k-space line is read after TR1, then after TR2, a fraction of a second apart.
  • So for each line the head is in the same place in both images: R2 = R1, and the ratio r = S2/S1 cancels the receive field. No motion between the images.
