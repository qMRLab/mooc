How to get every point of the quiz right

The magnetization starts along z and is tipped towards the right by the actual flip angle, 60° × B1 or 120° × B1. The signal is its transverse part (the bar below each vector). Dashed: B1 = 1. Move B1: which bar grows, which one shrinks?

  • Brighter at 60° → B1 > 1. Brighter at 120° → B1 < 1 (up to B1 = 1.5).
  • The receive coil scales both images by the same amount, so it can’t swap which one is brighter.

Exercise 4-1 · (b) · another pair of angles

Does it work for another pair of angles?

At 60° and 120°: brighter at 60° → B1 > 1, brighter at 120° → B1 < 1. Does that work for other angles? Pick two angles, then move B1 from 0.5 to 1.5: which image is brighter?

  • It works for any two angles that add up to 180° (e.g. 45° and 135°).
  • But the DAM formula, B1 = arccos(S2α/2Sα)/α, needs one angle twice the other.
  • Only 60° and 120° do both: α + 2α = 180° → α = 60°.

Exercise 4-1 · Summary

Guesstimating B1 from the raw data

  1. Learn to guess B1 from the raw images: it can help you at the scanner, before you can process the data.
  2. 60° and 120° are a special case: the only pair that does both
    1. gives the same signal at B1 = 1, so the brighter image tells you if B1 is above or below 1;
    2. fits the double-angle equation, B1 = arccos(S2α/2Sα)/α.
  3. This holds for any receive coil: the receive field multiplies both images by the same factor in each voxel, whatever the signal, so it can’t change which one is brighter, and it cancels in the ratio.
