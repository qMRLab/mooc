Exercise 4-1 · (b)

How to get every point of the quiz right

The magnetization starts along z and is tipped towards the right by the actual flip angle, 60° × B1 or 120° × B1. The signal is its transverse part (the bar below each vector). Dashed: B1 = 1. Move B1: which bar grows, which one shrinks?

120° pulse–
brighter–

At B1 = 1 the two vectors are mirror images about the horizontal: the same signal (0.87). B1 turns both further in the same direction. Above 1, the 60° vector moves towards the horizontal (more signal) and the 120° vector away from it (less). Below 1, the opposite: the 120° vector comes up towards the horizontal (and, below 0.75, past it, but it stays closer to it than the 60° vector). So the image whose vector is closer to the horizontal is brighter, and the two swap exactly at B1 = 1. This holds for any B1 up to 1.5, where the 120° vector reaches 180°; above that, its signal comes back.

Rule: at each point, compare the same pixel in the two images. Brighter at 60° → B1 > 1. Brighter at 120° → B1 < 1 (for any B1 below 1.5). The receive coil multiplies both images by the same number at that pixel, so it can make a point bright or dark, but it doesn’t change which of the two images is brighter.

Exercise 4-1 · (b) · another pair of angles

Would the rule work for another pair of angles?

The rule for 60° and 120°: brighter at 60° → B1 > 1, brighter at 120° → B1 < 1. Would it work with 45° and 135°? Move B1 from 0.5 to 1.5: which image is brighter?

Yes. sin 45° = sin 135°, so the two images are equal exactly at B1 = 1 and swap there, as for 60° and 120°: brighter at 45° → B1 > 1, brighter at 135° → B1 < 1.

For the rule, what matters is that the two angles add up to 180°, not that one is twice the other. But the DAM equation, B1 = arccos(S2α/2Sα)/α, needs the double angle: this pair needs its own, S135/S45 = 1 + 2 cos(90°·B1), so B1 = arccos((r − 1)/2)/90° (up to B1 = 1.33, where 135°·B1 passes 180°).

Only 60° and 120° are both: a double angle whose two angles add up to 180°. α + 2α = 180° → α = 60°.

Exercise 4-1 · Summary

Guesstimating B1 from the raw data

  1. Long TR: each image is S = R · M0 · sin(α·B1), with R the receive field. At 60° and 120° the two images are equal exactly where B1 = 1, because sin 60° = sin 120°.
  2. The rule: brighter at 60° → B1 > 1; brighter at 120° → B1 < 1 (for B1 up to 1.5).
  3. The receive field multiplies both images by the same number at each pixel: it can make a point bright or dark, but doesn’t change which image is brighter relative to the other, and it cancels in the ratio: S2α/2Sα = cos(α·B1), so B1 = arccos(S2α/2Sα)/α.
  4. Why 60° and 120°: the rule needs two angles that add up to 180°, the DAM equation needs the second to be twice the first. Only α = 60° does both. And near B1 = 1 both images have the same signal (0.87 M0), so the same SNR.
