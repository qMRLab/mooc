Exercise 6-4 · (d)

What did MTR measure?

Your six answers on the two curves. What do they say about MTR as a measure of macromolecules?

  • F = 0 is not MTR = 0. The pulse saturates water directly a little (~4 % here): a floor that depends on the pulse and on water's T2.
  • Not proportional to F. Doubling F from 10 to 20 % raises MTR by about a third, not twice: the saturation saturates.
  • Exchange matters as much as quantity. Halving kf costs as much MTR as removing a quarter of the macromolecules; with kf = 0 they are invisible, however many there are.
  • So MTR mixes F, kf (and T1, B1, the protocol): a lower MTR in a lesion can mean less myelin, or slower exchange, or longer T1.

Exercise 6-4 · Summary

Summary

  1. Each point of a Z-spectrum is an MTR measurement; the MTR protocol picks one offset and one pulse.
  2. MTR rises with F, but not in proportion, and it is not 0 without macromolecules (direct saturation).
  3. MTR depends on the exchange rate as much as on F: it is a mixture, not a measure of one tissue property.
