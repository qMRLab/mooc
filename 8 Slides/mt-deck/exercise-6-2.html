MTR vs MTsat: what changes them?

In this exercise, we will cover:

Halving the macromolecules: F halves, and since k_f = F·k_r with k_r (the exchange rate per macromolecular proton) a property of the molecules, k_f halves too. T1,f, T2s and R1,r unchanged. MTR drops by only about 21 % of its value and MTsat by about 40 %; neither halves. MTR is far from linear in the macromolecular content: water's T1 recovery and the readout limit how far it can move. MTsat is closer to proportional but not linear either. (Halving F alone at fixed k_f, which doubles k_r, would give drops of about 9 % and 22 %.) Tab 2, exchange halves: k_f halves at the same F (so k_r halves too): MTR drops by about 16 % (53.5 → 45.1 %) and MTsat by about 30 % (3.92 → 2.80 %): slower exchange passes less of the macromolecular saturation on to water.

The same simulated brain (each voxel's qMT fit, B1 = 1, no B0) with two MTsat protocols that differ only in the TR of the MT-weighted and PD-weighted images: Helms 2008 (TR 25 ms) and the same protocol at TR 40 ms, both with a Gaussian MT pulse of 540°, 12.8 ms at 2.2 kHz, 5° readouts, and the same T1-weighted image (15°, 11 ms). Brain medians: MTR 49.1 → 44.4 % (−9 %, lower), MTsat 2.54 → 3.17 % (+26 %, higher): they move in opposite directions. With a longer TR, water recovers more between MT pulses, so the MT-weighted signal sits closer to the PD-weighted one and MTR drops; MTsat, Helms' saturation per MT pulse after the T1 correction, rises (the readout's share of each TR's loss is smaller). (Campbell 2018, TR 30 ms, gives −3 % and +10 %.) Tab 2, the MT pulse at 270° instead of 540° (a quarter of the power): MTR 49.1 → 29.8 % (−38 %), MTsat 2.54 → 1.12 % (−55 %); both lower, MTsat roughly in proportion to the pulse power, MTR much less. Tab 3, the offset at 4.4 instead of 2.2 kHz: MTR 49.1 → 41.4 % (−16 %), MTsat 2.54 → 1.85 % (−26 %); both lower, as the macromolecular line absorbs less further out. Neither is a tissue property: both depend on the protocol, which is why values from different sites are hard to compare.

The same simulated brain and the Karakuzu 2022 Siemens protocol of the tissue slide, with one imperfection at a time. Tab 1, B1 = 0.8, no B1 map: every pulse tips 20 % less, the readouts and the MT pulse (540° → 432°), and MTR and MTsat are computed with the nominal flip angles. MTR 53.5 → 48.7 % (−9 %, lower): the weaker MT pulse saturates less. MTsat 3.92 → 4.78 % (+22 %, higher): the T1 and A that the MTsat equations take from the PDw and T1w images (VFA) carry the B1 error (T1 reads 0.8² = 0.64 times too short), and propagated through the equations they divide the saturation by about B1², more than the weaker pulse lowered it. Same direction as Figure 6.19 (uncorrected). Tab 2, the same B1 with a B1 map: the readout angles are corrected (4.8° and 16°), but the MT pulse's angle is not in Helms' equations. MTR unchanged (−9 %, it has no angles in it); MTsat 3.92 → 3.06 % (−22 %, lower): the weaker MT pulse really saturates less, and nothing in the calculation undoes it. That leftover is what the empirical correction (Helms; Weiskopf 2013, λ ≈ 0.4) addresses. Tab 3, B0 100 Hz off, towards the MT pulse (1.2 kHz becomes 1.1 kHz from water): MTR +2.6 % (about the same), MTsat +5.5 % (just past our 5 % line: slightly higher). Off-resonance errors of this size matter little at 1.2 kHz; they matter more for qMT points close to resonance. Tab 4, T1: the dataset's T1 map reads about 980 ms in white matter (median of voxels with F > 18 %), in the VFA range of Stikov et al. 2015 (Look-Locker about 650 ms, IR about 830 ms, VFA about 1000 ms in WM). If the tissue's T1 were really the IR one, 17 % shorter (R1,f recomputed with Eq. 6.6): MTR 53.5 → 48.9 % (−9 %, lower), MTsat 3.92 → 3.84 % (−2 %, about the same). MTsat's T1 correction does its job; MTR does not have one.

Exercise 6-2 · Summary

Summary

  1. Neither MTR nor MTsat is proportional to the macromolecular content: halving the macromolecules lowers MTR by about 20 % and MTsat by about 40 % (halving exchange: 15 % and 30 %).
  2. Both depend on the protocol. A weaker MT pulse or a larger offset lowers both; a longer TR lowers MTR but raises MTsat. Compare values only within one protocol.
  3. MTsat removes most of the T1 dependence (−2 % against −9 % for MTR, for a 17 % shorter T1), but not B1: at B1 = 0.8 it reads 22 % high without a B1 map and 22 % low with one. Empirical corrections (Helms, Lipp) address the rest, with protocol-specific constants.
  4. A B0 offset of 100 Hz barely matters at a 1.2 kHz MT offset (a few %).
