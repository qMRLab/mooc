So: usable at 1.5 T, biased by about 6% at 3 T.

As in Figure 2.3. The slide opens on the magnitude signal of the general equation: what the scanner measures, for WM at 1.5 T and TR = 3 s. Click 1: the long-TR model, what the fit assumes. Click 2: where the difference matters. They differ by a constant, e^(−TR/T1) ≈ 0.01, which matters most where the signal is small: around the null. The true null is at T1 ln[2/(1 + e^(−TR/T1))] ≈ 444 ms; the model's is at T1 ln 2 ≈ 451 ms.

c. Which inversion times to avoid

A protocol of three TIs, 100 ms apart. Slide it along the recovery and fit each position with the long-TR model (noise-free data, WM at 1.5 T, TR = 3 s).

Avoid sampling mostly around the null (≈ 450 ms), where the model error is large relative to the signal, or far past it (≳ 2.5 T1), where the curve is flat. Spread the TIs across the recovery, and keep TI below TR minus the readout.

Three clicks. 1: the sweep: each point is a long-TR fit by qmrust to three TIs (centre ± 100 ms) of noise-free data. 2: the problem zones, marked. 3: the answer. Near the null the fit error swings to ±20–30%; from about 1.6 s on, the three samples lie on the plateau and the fit cannot find T1 at all (it runs to its bounds). Spread-out protocols keep the bias near the 1.7% of part b. This is a proposed answer: discuss what else the class would avoid (for example, TIs too close together).
