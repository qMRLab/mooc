At TR = 3 s the long-TR fit underestimates WM T1 by about 1.7% at 1.5 T (TR/T1 = 4.6) and about 5.8% at 3 T (TR/T1 = 3.3). The neglected term is e^(−TR/T1): 0.010 at 1.5 T and 0.036 at 3 T. So: usable at 1.5 T, biased by about 6% at 3 T.

c. TIs to avoid: Figure 2.6

c. Are there any inversion times you should avoid using when designing an IR T1 mapping protocol at 1.5T when using this TR?

Avoid TIs at the null of the tissue you want to measure: magnitude images there are dominated by Rician noise, which can negatively impact the fit under low SNR. For WM at 1.5 T with TR = 3 s, that is TI ≈ 444 ms.

The slide opens on Figure 2.6 as the book publishes it: Monte Carlo simulations for T1 = 900 ms with six TIs linearly spaced up to TR; the insets give the mean and STD of the fitted T1 against TR. Click 1: the slider moves to TR = 2900 ms, where the second TI (TR/5 = 580 ms) lands on the null (589 ms). Both are circled: that TI on the signal curve, and the STD of the fitted T1 in the inset, which blips up there (about 90 ms, against about 70–75 ms for nearby TRs). The simulations are noisy, so other TRs wobble too; drag the slider around 2600–3000 ms to show it. Click 2: the answer, from the book's text: avoid TIs where the signal of the tissue of interest is nulled. For WM at 1.5 T (650 ms) with TR = 3 s, the null is at 444 ms (Eq. 2.2; 451 ms with the long-TR approximation, as in part a).
