Variable flip angle: the Ernst angle and contrast

  1. a. Using the Plotly figure in the VFA section that displays the signal curves for white matter, gray matter, and CSF, determine the Ernst angle for the following: white matter and a TR of 15 ms, gray matter for a TR of 50 ms, and CSF for a TR of 140 ms.
  2. b. Calculate the Ernst angle for these tissues and TRs with Eq. 2.6. Are they in agreement with the values you estimated with the interactive figure?
  3. c. It’s been shown (Deoni, Rutt, and Peters 2003) that the optimal flip angles for a two-measurement VFA T1 mapping imaging protocol are located at approximately 70% of the Ernst angle. Find the optimal flip angles to image each tissue above using their respective TRs.*
  4. d. For a TR of 30 ms, which flip angle would result in the best contrast between white matter and grey matter in an SPGR image?

* Deoni et al. place the two angles where the signal is about 71% of the Ernst signal, one on each side of the Ernst angle. Part c is solved that way.

Exercise 2 from the end of the T1 chapter, quoted as written. Correction for part c: Deoni, Rutt and Peters (2003) show the two optimal angles are those giving about 71% of the maximum (Ernst) signal, one below and one above the Ernst angle; not 70% of the Ernst angle itself. Taken literally, 0.7 × θE would give 7.3° (WM), 10.3° (GM) and 10.5° (CSF), a single angle each.

c. Optimal flip angles for two-point VFA

c. It’s been shown (Deoni, Rutt, and Peters 2003) that the optimal flip angles for a two-measurement VFA T1 mapping imaging protocol are located at approximately 70% of the Ernst angle. Find the optimal flip angles to image each tissue above using their respective TRs.

Deoni et al.: two angles at 71% of the Ernst signal, one each side of θE. Not one angle at 0.7 θE ().

Three clicks. 1: each tissue's signal (Eq. 2.5 at its TR) divided by its value at the Ernst angle, so all three peak at 1. 2: the two angles where each curve crosses 0.71 are the optimal pair: WM 4.4° and 24.7°, GM 6.2° and 34.4°, CSF 6.3° and 35.2°. 3: note the asymmetry, and compare with the exercise's literal "70% of the Ernst angle" (7.3°, 10.3°, 10.5°), which is a misreading of the paper.
