Setting Eq. 2.3 to zero:
$$0 = C\left(1-2e^{- \frac{TI_{null}}{T_1}}\right) \;\Rightarrow\; TI_{null} = T_1 \ln 2 \approx 0.69\,T_1$$
Type in the class's readings if they differ. Solve fits a straight line through the origin, TI_null = k·T1. The slope comes out at ln 2 ≈ 0.693, which is what setting Eq. 2.3 to zero gives. This holds for the long-TR approximation (Eq. 2.3) used in Figure 2.2. With a finite TR (Eq. 2.2), the null comes slightly earlier.

c. Lowest WM/GM contrast in magnitude images

c. Assuming that the images acquired at the MRI scanner displays magnitude-only images, at approximately which inversion time will the white matter and grey matter have the lowest contrast?

Between the two nulls, |Mz| of WM and GM cross at TI ≈ 800 ms: the two tissues look the same.

The crossing solves e−TI/T1,WM + e−TI/T1,GM = 1, with the T1 values of Figure 2.2.

First click: the same book data shown as magnitude, which is what a magnitude image displays. Second click: the WM and GM magnitudes cross at about 797 ms, so they have no contrast there. Signed (real) images would still separate them, because WM is positive and GM negative at that TI.

d. Best WM/GM contrast

d. Which inversion time would result in the best contrast between white matter and grey matter?

The difference peaks at TI ≈ 1150 ms.

$$TI_{best} = \frac{T_{1,WM}\,T_{1,GM}}{T_{1,GM}-T_{1,WM}}\ln\frac{T_{1,GM}}{T_{1,WM}}$$

From differentiating Eq. 2.3 for WM minus GM. Both tissues are positive there, so magnitude images give the same answer.

First click adds the curve Mz(WM) − Mz(GM) to the book figure. Second click marks its maximum, about 1149 ms for T1 of 900 and 1500 ms. Setting the derivative of e^(−TI/T1,GM) − e^(−TI/T1,WM) to zero gives the formula.
