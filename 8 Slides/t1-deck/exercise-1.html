Setting Eq. 2.3 to zero:
$$\begin{aligned} 0 &= C\left(1-2e^{- \frac{TI_{null}}{T_1}}\right) \;\Rightarrow\; e^{- \frac{TI_{null}}{T_1}} = \frac{1}{2} \\ TI_{null} &= T_1 \ln 2 \approx 0.693\,T_1 \end{aligned}$$
Type in the class's readings if they differ. Three clicks. 1: the readings plotted against T1: they fall on a straight line through the origin. 2: a least-squares line through the origin, TI_null = k·T1, gives k ≈ 0.693. 3: proof: setting Eq. 2.3 to zero gives TI_null = T1 ln 2, and ln 2 = 0.693. This holds for the long-TR approximation (Eq. 2.3) used in Figure 2.2. With a finite TR (Eq. 2.2), the null comes slightly earlier.

c. Lowest WM/GM contrast in magnitude images

c. Assuming that the images acquired at the MRI scanner displays magnitude-only images, at approximately which inversion time will the white matter and grey matter have the lowest contrast?

Between the two nulls, |Mz| of WM and GM cross at TI ≈ 800 ms: the two tissues look the same.

The crossing solves e−TI/T1,WM + e−TI/T1,GM = 1, with the T1 values of Figure 2.2.

Two clicks. 1: the same book data shown as magnitude, which is what a magnitude image displays. 2: the WM and GM magnitudes cross at about 797 ms, so they have no contrast there. Signed (real) images would still separate them, because WM is positive and GM negative at that TI.

d. Best WM/GM contrast in magnitude images

d. Which inversion time would result in the best contrast between white matter and grey matter?

The magnitude contrast peaks at TI ≈ 1150 ms.

Both tissues are past their null there, so |Mz| = Mz. Setting the derivative of Eq. 2.3 for WM minus GM to zero:

$$\begin{aligned} &\frac{d}{dTI}\left(e^{-\frac{TI}{T_{1,GM}}} - e^{-\frac{TI}{T_{1,WM}}}\right) = 0 \\ &\Rightarrow\; TI_{best} = \frac{T_{1,WM}\,T_{1,GM}}{T_{1,GM}-T_{1,WM}}\ln\frac{T_{1,GM}}{T_{1,WM}} \end{aligned}$$

The slide opens on the book figure as magnitude, |Mz|, as in part c. Three clicks. 1: adds the WM/GM contrast, ||Mz(WM)| − |Mz(GM)||. It is zero at the crossing of part c, has a smaller peak at the WM null (0.32 at 624 ms) and its highest point past the GM null. 2: marks its maximum, about 1149 ms (0.37) for T1 of 900 and 1500 ms. The curve is flat between the GM null and about 1300 ms, so any TI there gives nearly the best contrast. 3: proof: both tissues are positive there, so magnitude equals Mz, and setting the derivative of e^(−TI/T1,GM) − e^(−TI/T1,WM) to zero gives the formula.
