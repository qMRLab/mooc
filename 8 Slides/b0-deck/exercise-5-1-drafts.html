Water, fat and B0: when to take your echoes, and how many

Exercise 5-1 · (b)

How would you suppress the fat signal in this image?

T1-weighted GRE, 3 T (TR 20 ms, 25°). Scalp and marrow fat are bright.

Fat is bright on T1-weighted images and can hide what you want to see. What do you know about fat that sets it apart from water? Think of two or three ways to get rid of its signal.

  • Its T1 is short (≈ 380 ms at 3 T): invert, and image when fat crosses zero (STIR). → (c)
  • It precesses 3.4 ppm slower than water: saturate only its frequency (fat sat). → (d)
  • So its phase drifts from water’s with TE: separate the two from echoes at chosen TEs (Dixon). → (g), (h)
The simulated image uses the 2013 Stikov 3 T slice: measured T1 in the brain, the head segmentation (brain, bone, scalp, fat), and the measured B1 map. Fat: T1 = 380 ms; scalp is taken as mostly fat (subcutaneous), bone as fatty marrow. The three answers follow the lecture notes' T1-based, frequency-based and phase-based families.

STIR magnitude signal with TR ≫ T1: |1 − (1 − cos θ)e^(−TI/T1)|, θ = 180° × B1, times the proton density. With a perfect inversion fat nulls at T1 ln 2; with the measured B1 (0.7–1.25 in this slice) the null moves and fat leaves a residue, more in the scalp where B1 is lowest. Insensitive to B0 though (lecture notes: STIR pros).

Here the field is perfectly shimmed. An ideal rectangular band (with 15 Hz edges) saturates fat at −434 Hz. Fat is modelled as a single peak; real fat also has smaller peaks (one close to water), so even a perfect fat sat leaves about 15 % of the fat signal (lecture notes, slide 10). Water excitation is the mirror image of this.

  • The field shifts water and fat together, voxel by voxel. On one side fat moves out of the band and stays bright; on the other, water moves into it and goes dark.
  • It only takes a field about as large as the band’s margins, a few hundred Hz, which is common at the edges of the neck, the shoulders or near implants.
The field here is a simulated left-right gradient (from −X/2 to +X/2 across the head), as from a poor linear shim; the lecture's own B0 map of this slice is much smaller (±30 Hz) because the head is well shimmed. The spectrum of the slice broadens as the field spreads. Lecture notes, slides 9 and 15.

Exercise 5-1 · (f) · predict first

You have a B0 map. Can it fix the fat sat?

The head of (e), with a field that varies across it, and a perfect map of that field.

  1. Before the scan: can the B0 map make the fat sat work everywhere? The fat-sat pulse saturates one band of frequencies for the whole slice: it can’t follow the field voxel by voxel. Shifting it fixes one side and breaks the other. The map helps prospectively only through shimming, and shims only cancel smooth fields. See it on the previous slide: move the field, and think about where you would put the band.
  2. After the scan: can the B0 map undo the fat that wasn’t saturated? No: in each voxel that fat is now part of one signal with the water. One image gives no way to split them, whatever you know about the field.

Exercise 5-1 · (g)

Use phase: 2-point Dixon, in phase + opposed

Water = (IP + OP)/2

Fat = (IP − OP)/2

Two echoes: in phase (IP, 2.30 ms) and opposed (OP, 1.15 ms). Water adds the same way in both; fat flips sign. Sum and difference give water and fat. Does it survive the field of (e)?

Field across the head
Fat in the water image
Field phase between OP and IP, at the edge
  • Sum and difference only work if the field adds no phase between the two echoes.
  • A field ψ turns OP by 2πψ × 1.15 ms relative to IP: water and fat mix, half and half at ±217 Hz, and swap at ±434 Hz.
  • To correct it you need the field, but two echoes can’t also give you a field map. What would a third echo add?
Echoes at 1.15 and 2.30 ms (fat at −180° and 0° from water at 3 T, single peak). Water = (IP + OP)/2, fat = (IP − OP)/2, as complex images (lecture notes slides 20-23). The error is the field's phase between the echoes, 2πψ × 1.15 ms. Modern 2-point methods estimate that phase with extra assumptions (a smooth field), which is where water-fat swaps come from.

B0 map error (RMS, head)
  • Echoes 1 and 3, 2.30 ms apart, give the field alone: a B0 map, ψ = ∠(S3S1*) / (2π × 2.30 ms).
  • Remove that phase from IP and OP, then sum and difference: water and fat separate cleanly.
  • The catch: a 2.30 ms gap wraps past ±217 Hz. Push the field to see it, then unwrap the map, or use IDEAL’s uneven echoes.
Glover's 3-point Dixon (1991), lecture notes slides 24-26: echoes at −π, 0 and +π fat-water angle. Echoes 1 and 3 have the same fat angle, so ∠(S3·S1*) = 2πψ × 2.30 ms: the field (here without unwrapping, so it wraps past ±217 Hz). The phasors show one scalp voxel at the field's highest edge. IDEAL (Reeder 2004) generalises this to any three TEs with a least-squares fit, and adds multi-peak fat and T2*.
