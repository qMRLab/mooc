The scanner’s frequency was set wrong: what does the field map show?

Exercise 5-3 · (a)

A perfect field, a wrong frequency

A homogeneous phantom in a perfectly uniform field of exactly 3 T, on a 3 T scanner. By mistake, the imaging frequency (f0) was set to that of 3 T − 4.69731 µT. You run a dual-echo GRE with ΔTE = 5 ms.

First, convert: how far off resonance are the spins, in Hz?

Hz

The chapter's exercise 3. γ/2π = 42.58 MHz/T, so 4.69731 µT is 200.0 Hz (and 2.35 µT, in part (b) of the book's exercise, is 100 Hz).

Exercise 5-3 · (b) · predict first

What will the field map show?

The phantom of (a): a perfectly uniform field, f0 set too low, dual-echo GRE with ΔTE = 5 ms, a little noise.

  1. f0 is 200 Hz too low. What does the field map show? 0 Hz: in 5 ms, 200 Hz turns the phase exactly once (360°), so both echoes have the same phase. The map looks perfectly shimmed. Check it on the next slide, then come back: your progress here is kept.
  2. If f0 were 2.35 µT too low instead (100 Hz), what would the map show? In 5 ms, 100 Hz turns the phase by exactly 180°: the phase difference sits on the ±π edge, and noise pushes each voxel to one side or the other, +100 or −100 Hz at random. Check it on the next slide, then come back: your progress here is kept.
  3. With ΔTE = 5 ms, which of these offsets does the map show correctly? Only offsets within ±1/(2ΔTE) = ±100 Hz: 80 Hz is right, but 120 Hz reads as −80 Hz, 180 Hz as −20 Hz and 250 Hz as +50 Hz. Check it on the next slide, then come back: your progress here is kept.

Exercise 5-3 · (d)

The map looks perfect. How would you catch it?

The phantom of (a): f0 200 Hz too low, ΔTE = 5 ms, so the map reads 0 Hz. Predict first: which of these would show the 200 Hz?

Mean of the map
  • Spatial unwrapping can’t: a uniform offset has no wraps in space to find. The map stays at 0 Hz.
  • A short echo spacing can: 1 ms gives ±500 Hz without wraps, coarse but unambiguous. It then tells how many turns the 5 ms pair made.
  • At the scanner: check the frequency adjustment. The water peak should sit at f0.
The third echo is the lecture's "close pair, then a late echo" (Figure 5.16): the 1 ms pair estimates the offset coarsely without wraps (±500 Hz), which gives the number of turns of the 5 ms pair; the 5 ms pair then refines it. Spatial unwrapping only finds 2π jumps between neighbouring voxels, and a uniform offset has none.

Exercise 5-3 · Summary

A wrong f0 in a field map

  1. A wrong f0 adds the same offset everywhere: 4.69731 µT is 200 Hz at 3 T.
  2. The map can hide it: with ΔTE = 5 ms, 200 Hz is exactly one turn and reads 0 Hz, a perfectly shimmed map. 100 Hz is half a turn and flips between ±100 Hz with noise.
  3. Spatial unwrapping can’t see it (there are no wraps in space); a shorter echo spacing can, which is one reason for multi-echo field maps.
