  • They don’t have to be in phase: fat only needs the same angle to water at both echoes. That’s ΔTE = n × 2.30 ms at 3 T, for any TE1.
  • In phase at both is the usual choice (it also avoids signal cancellation), but opposed at both (1.15 and 3.45 ms) works too. Keep that in mind for (h).
  • Any other ΔTE counts fat’s chemical shift as field, worst in voxels that are half fat.
Signal model: S(t) = (W + F·e^(i2π·f_F·t))·e^(i2πψt), f_F = −434 Hz (−3.4 ppm at 3 T, single fat peak). The two-point field is ∠(S2·S1*)/(2πΔTE). The fat term changes the phase of the sum unless it has the same angle relative to water at both echoes, i.e. f_F·ΔTE is a whole number of turns. TE1 alone doesn't matter for the bias; it sets where in the cycle both echoes sit (and the SNR). The chapter's "ΔTE ≈ 2.34 ms at 3 T" and the 2.24 ms of the lecture's slide 2 map are this.
